# Blind item gallery comparison

Evidence: VALID

Winner: A

clear_advantage: true

Scope: Both supplied gallery pages in A and B, including all 134 displayed item states, their large and small icons, and the displayed cooldown/dimmed examples. This is a comparison of the project baselines against the supplied criteria, not a commercial superiority claim or an integration acceptance.

Isolation: Inspected only the images in the assigned A and B folders. No source, prompts, mappings, logs, or other project material was inspected.

Completeness: All 134 expected cells are visibly populated in each set (112 on page 1 and 22 on page 2). Every populated cell has its accompanying small icon. No missing, corrupt, or placeholder-looking cells were observed. The unused area on page 2 is outside the populated gallery.

A has a clear overall advantage in physical object recognition and dimensional materials. Heavy Belt reads as leather and metal rather than B's hanging pendant; Headband is a metal band rather than B's crown; Ripe Mango is visibly a mango rather than B's green heart. Chain Shirt, Fur Hat, Gold Glove, Net Staff, Teleport Scroll, War Drum, and the various rings also have recognizable objects, thickness, surface texture, and directional highlights. The painted treatment is consistent across equipment, consumables, and combination items.

B has cleaner flat shapes and less background detail at small size. Its empty space around weapons and its simple mode markers help quick reading. However, several physical objects become generic symbols or substantially different objects. That makes the set less dependable against the displayed names despite its consistent graphic style.

Observed pointers:

- Page 1, rows 1–3: A separates leather belt, chain shirt, metal band, feather, fur hat, gloves, and axe through shape and material. B keeps these readable as simple graphics, but several shapes do not match the named object closely.
- Page 1, rows 4 and 8, and page 2, rows 1–2: A shows the red sword, green curved sword, and blue open spellbook clearly enough to identify the required Kugmo, Igit, and Atuli objects. The combination icons retain the appropriate paired objects and colors. B also preserves these basic combinations.
- Page 2, row 2: Both sets distinguish the red, green, and blue Switch Boots modes. A gives the boot substantial physical form; B gives the modes a cleaner common silhouette.
- Page 1 Flask and page 2 Empty Flask: A's filled blue bottle and clear bottle visibly differ. B's Empty Flask still contains prominent green liquid and looks too close to its filled Flask.
- Small icons and overlays: A retains useful broad color and object cues for shields, armor, boots, books, rings, and most weapons. Fine decoration and bright effects compete with silhouettes on some staves and charms. B's simplified forms generally survive the reduction more cleanly. The displayed white cooldown numerals and diagonal dimming remain visible in both sets, although the numerals hide considerable object detail at the large-icon center.

Largest remaining gap: A's Blast Rod level differentiation. The five states occupy page 1, row 2's final three cells and row 3's first two cells. The rods look nearly identical, and no ascending power pips are visibly readable. B has ascending dot markers, giving the player an immediate level cue that A lacks.

Recheck: Reopened both page 1 images at original resolution and compared all five Blast Rod cells and their small companions again. The concern persists: A does not visibly communicate an ascending pip sequence; B does. Add a consistent, high-contrast pip sequence to A, then recheck all five levels at both supplied sizes with and without the shown dimming/cooldown treatment.
